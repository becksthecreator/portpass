# Lead funnel audit (PortPass Bahamas)

PortPass's core idea: customers book directly on the PortPass site, taking WhatsApp and DMs out of the booking process. Every audit points to that.

Sources: Oren John's funnels video (the "Funnels, Explained" deck) and the search videos in the "Get Found, Get Booked" deck (Dooley, Whitworth, SEO course).

## The funnel (4 steps)
1. SEE (top of funnel): how people find out the business exists. Ads, Instagram, Google search, AI answers, word of mouth.
2. CONSIDER (middle): how they check it out. Photos, prices, reviews, a page.
3. DECIDE (bottom): how they book and pay. This is where hot leads convert or leak.
4. COME BACK: rebooking, upsells and referrals.
Every step leaks people. Fix the biggest leak before adding more ads.

## Words to use
- Landing page.
- Offer: "___ that does ___ for ___".
- Lead and CRM.
- Lead magnet.
- Upsell, extras, guarantee.
- Tripwire: a cheap first sale that pays back ad spend.
- DM funnel: most of these businesses run one by hand, and that is the leak.
- Speed to lead: how fast a reply comes. James Dooley's sales team aims for 23 seconds.

## Steps
1. Read what Antonio shared (screenshot, chat, ad). Note prices, packages, requirements (ID, deposit, delivery) and how bookings happen today.
2. Research the business: web search the name plus Bahamas/Nassau. Check:
   - whether search finds them or a same-name business elsewhere;
   - whether they have a Google Business Profile, and its reviews;
   - number of locations or units;
   - whether they have a website.
   If you can, ask an AI assistant "best [service] in Nassau" and note whether they appear.
3. Map their funnel: one line per step saying what happens today. Include how fast a reply came if Antonio messaged them (auto-reply, minutes, hours).
4. Name the biggest leak and which step it is in. Common ones:
   - SEE: search shows the wrong business, or they're invisible on Google and in AI answers.
   - CONSIDER: no page with prices and photos.
   - DECIDE: the ad lands on an auto-reply or a slow DM.
   - DECIDE: repeated "is this date free?" questions.
   - DECIDE: details like ID or address collected by DM.
   - COME BACK: no follow-up or rebooking.
5. Show the PortPass fix for that leak: book on the site with package, date, details and ID upload; an instant confirmation; and their ads pointed at their PortPass page, so the visits also lift their search rankings.
6. Add the upsell: what the business, or another PortPass vendor, could offer right after booking. Cross-vendor upsell chains are a key PortPass selling point.
7. Flag fit and risks:
   - booking value (low-value bookings suggest a flat monthly fee);
   - stock and availability tracking;
   - overlap with The Beckfords' own businesses;
   - how the business will record payments (cash or bank transfer; PortPass never takes or holds the money and has no card payments).
8. Draft the opening message. Introduce Antonio from PortPass Bahamas, name their leak in plain words ("your ad is working; you're losing people in the DMs"), describe booking on PortPass, and ask for a quick chat. If Antonio first contacted them as a customer, be upfront about who he is.
9. Advise on timing, e.g. wait for a human reply after an auto-reply, and send by a set day if no answer.

## Output
- Lead with a one-line verdict: strong / good / weaker fit.
- Short sections: what we know, their funnel and leak, the fix and upsell, why they fit, things to watch, the message.
- If Antonio wants to understand it, make a short slides deck with a today-vs-PortPass funnel diagram in the PortPass navy/red palette.
- Never invent statistics. Label example numbers as examples.
